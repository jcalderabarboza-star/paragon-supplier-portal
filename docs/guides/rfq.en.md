## 1 · What this process is

A sourcing event: Paragon asks several suppliers for an offer on the same requirement, so the comparison is like for like. The RFQ is the buyer's document. It names what is being sourced (a title, a material category, one or more material codes, a total quantity and unit), who is invited, when responses are due, when the award is due, and on what commercial terms (Incoterms, payment terms, currency). Suppliers never edit it; they answer it with a quotation, which is a separate document with its own guide.

One role drives it end to end: Paragon's **buyer** (lane `procurement`) drafts the event, publishes it to the invited suppliers, records the exchange-rate basis when bids arrive in more than one currency, and finally awards it to one quotation, or cancels it. A closed event can be reopened by the same role. The supplier's **sales contact** (lane `commercial`) only sees the event once it is published, and only if invited; their act — submitting a quotation — is described in the quotation guide. Nobody presses the close step: it is declared as the response window ending and nothing in this build fires it.

It starts at **Draft**, becomes visible to suppliers at **Open**, and ends at **Awarded**, **Concluded** or **Cancelled**, the three terminal states. **Closed** is an event the buyer has closed to new quotations (**Close bidding**); it can still be awarded, reopened or cancelled. Awarding fans out onto the quotations under the platform's automation grant: the chosen quotation becomes Awarded and every other quotation on the same event becomes Rejected, in the same act. Cancelling fans out too: every quotation still being weighed becomes Withdrawn.

**Stages.** An event may run in up to three stages — **RFI** (request for information), **RFP** (request for proposal) and **RFQ** (request for quotation) — and is one document throughout. The buyer chooses the stage it starts at in the wizard; an event that states none is at RFQ, which is every event raised before stages existed. The stage is a field on the event, not a state: at each stage the event is Draft, Open or Closed as before. At RFI a supplier answers the event's **questionnaire** — written by the buyer on the draft, before publishing — or, when the event asks none, records its interest and a note; at RFP it submits a **proposal** against the event's **evaluation criteria** — weighted, written by the buyer on the draft — or, when the event sets none, records its interest and a note (see the stage-response guide). Once bidding on the RFP is closed, named evaluators score each proposal per criterion and the buyer reads the ranking; RFI answers are not scored. The buyer closes bidding on a stage and then **advances** the event with a shortlist of the suppliers who answered: the invite list becomes the shortlist, and a supplier left out reads "Not shortlisted" with the buyer's reason. An award is made only at the RFQ stage. At any stage the buyer may **conclude without award**, stating why; that is the terminal state **Concluded**.

Honesty markers a reader needs before using this guide. The demo RFQs and quotations are **SIMULATED** fixtures; the declared present the buyer board measures deadlines against is **31 Aug 2026**, and the sourcing fixtures are anchored to it, so the seeded Open events read as due within days of it and exactly one — RFQ-2026-010 — reads as overdue; the Draft the platform raises for itself at boot has its deadline in November 2026. An award records the winning quotation and supplier on the RFQ and **mints nothing downstream** — no purchase order, no contract; the supplier's award tab honestly shows "PO issued —". An exchange-rate pin is recorded with liveness `SIMULATED` whether typed by hand or labelled as an SAP rate, because no rate feed is connected. A seat opens with nobody named (`UNATTRIBUTED: NO_PERSON_IN_SESSION`); publishing, cancelling and awarding are refused on such a seat and need a sample user adopted on the identity panel, while creating a draft, closing bidding, reopening and recording a rate do not. The two rules that gate publication — invitee eligibility and the competition floor — are real and refuse at the machine; the exemption that lifts the floor is read from the preferred-supplier list, which is itself seeded at boot.

**Forks**

- **At Draft:** `t_rfq_questionnaire_set` — procurement — when the event starts at RFI and every invited supplier should be asked the same questions (the RFQ stays Draft); `t_rfq_criteria_set` — procurement — when the event has an RFP stage and its proposals should be weighed on stated criteria (the RFQ stays Draft); `t_rfq_publish` — procurement — when the invited list is settled and the event should go in front of the suppliers; `t_rfq_cancel` — procurement — when the requirement or the budget went away before anyone was asked.
- **At Open:** `t_rfq_award` — procurement — when at least one quotation has been received and one is chosen (not every invitee need have answered); `t_rfq_fx_pin` — procurement — when bids arrived in more than one currency and the comparison needs a recorded rate (the RFQ stays Open); `t_rfq_cancel` — procurement — when the event is called off before an award; `t_rfq_close` — procurement — when the buyer stops taking quotations.
- **At Closed:** `t_rfq_proposal_score` — procurement — at the RFP stage of an event that sets criteria, when an evaluator scores a submitted proposal (the RFQ stays Closed); `t_rfq_reopen` — procurement — when more responses are wanted or the requirement moved; `t_rfq_award` — procurement — legal in the machine, but the buyer surface offers the award panel only on an Open event, so in practice reopen comes first; `t_rfq_fx_pin` — procurement — a rate may still be recorded (the RFQ stays Closed); `t_rfq_cancel` — procurement — when the closed event will not be awarded.

## 3 · Step by step

### t_rfq_create — Save an RFQ draft <!-- transition:t_rfq_create -->

- **Step kind:** operator action (creation); cascade source (`t_pr_source` on the requisition it was raised from, when one was chosen)
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:create`)
- **From → to:** ∅ → Draft
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` ("Sourcing events") → header button **New sourcing event** → the four-step wizard **Define Scope** · **Invite Suppliers** · **Terms & Deadlines** · **Review & Submit** → **Save event draft**. A seat without `rfq:create` sees "Awaiting Procurement" in the button's slot instead of the button.
- **Operator — do:** Buying starts shaping a request — what is needed, in what category, how much — while it is still internal. Step 1: optionally pick an approved requisition under "Raise from requisition" (it fills what it can; the review step lists what was "Not carried over"), type the RFQ title, choose the material category, tick the specific material(s), type the total quantity (digits only — "e.g. 2400") and unit, and optionally an estimated budget in IDR. Step 2: tick the suppliers to invite; the step refuses to advance while the invited set would fail publication (an ineligible invitee, or fewer than two eligible invitees without an exemption) and the same notice the publish step will give is shown here, while the list is still editable. Step 3: response deadline, award deadline (must be after the response deadline), optionally a requested delivery date (when stated, it must be after the award deadline), Incoterms (the list opens on CIF), payment terms, currency. Step 4: review and press Save RFQ draft. Toast: "RFQ-2026-9xx saved as a draft · Not sent yet — publish to open it to N invited suppliers".
- **Operator — fill:** the title (`title`), the material category (`materialCategory`) and the total quantity (`totalQty`) are required by the machine; the wizard additionally requires at least one material, a valid invited set, and both deadlines. The quantity and the budget go through one parser: a blank quantity, a non-number, or an ambiguous "2.400" is refused with its own message; a blank budget is stored as absent ("Not specified"), never as Rp 0. *Start at stage* (step 1) — RFI, RFP or RFQ; RFQ is preselected. On step 3 a response deadline that has already passed is refused on the field ("This date has already passed…") and the step does not advance. *Requested delivery date (optional)* (step 3) — a date on or before the award deadline is refused on the field ("A requested delivery date must be after the award deadline.") and the step does not advance; left blank, no date is stored (`requestedDeliveryDate` is written only when stated).
- **Tester — expected state:** Draft
- **Tester — confirm:** the new row appears on the board (visible under the **All** tab only — Draft matches no other group), with a store-assigned number in the `RFQ-2026-9xx` range and status pill **Draft**; opening it shows **Publish event** and **Cancel event** under "Lifecycle actions" and the preferred-supplier gate notice for the event. On the supplier side nothing appears yet: the supplier read excludes Draft. If a requisition was chosen, `/buyer/purchase-requisition` now shows that requisition in **Sourcing Event** with the new RFQ number as its linked document. The review step and the event panel show "Requested delivery" with the stated date, or "Not stated". Once the event is published, the supplier's card reads "Req. Delivery:" with that date, or "not stated".
- **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_create` (and, when raised from an approved requisition, a cascaded `t_pr_source` on the requisition with `causationId` = the create's correlationId)
- **Checks that can refuse:** none beyond role, legality and required fields. Scope: RFQ creation is a buyer act — a supplier seat is refused `SCOPE_DENIED` before the role gate, because the RFQ target has no supplier owner. The wizard's own gates stop most bad input first: an unreadable quantity produces "Event not created — check the numbers". One hook: `rfq_create_stage_known` — a stated `stage` that is not RFI, RFP or RFQ is refused `STAGE_UNKNOWN`; an absent one starts the event at RFQ.
- **Glossary:** `MISSING_FIELDS`, `SCOPE_DENIED`, `EMPTY_QTY`, `NOT_NUMERIC`, `AMBIGUOUS_QTY`.
- **Honesty:** Saving does not send anything — the label used to read as if it did and was corrected. The invited list is written once, at creation; no verb adds or removes an invitee afterwards, which is why the wizard refuses an unpublishable set at step 2 rather than letting publish refuse it later. A material picked without a master code puts no code on the event: the event then cannot be checked for a preferred-supplier exemption and will require competitive bidding (the wizard says so and offers to raise a material request after the RFQ exists). The platform itself raises one Draft through this verb at boot — `RFQ-2026-901`, the material-request seed's event — which is why a fresh session already shows a Draft that is not in the fixture file. The Incoterms shown on step 3 are the Incoterms stored: the list opens on CIF, one of its own options. The requested delivery date is the only delivery date a supplier is shown — the supplier's card never borrows the award deadline for it, and no seeded event states one.

### t_rfq_questionnaire_set — Write the RFI questionnaire <!-- transition:t_rfq_questionnaire_set -->

- **Step kind:** records a fact (state-preserving)
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:create`)
- **From → to:** Draft → Draft
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` → open a **Draft** event that starts at RFI → section **RFI questionnaire** → **Write questionnaire** (or **Edit questionnaire** once one is saved) → the editor → **Save questionnaire** (or **Cancel**, which saves nothing). The section is absent on an event that starts at RFP or RFQ. A seat without `rfq:create` sees "Awaiting Procurement" in the button's slot (`handoff-rfq-questionnaire`).
- **Operator — do:** Write what every invited supplier will be asked at the RFI stage. **Add question**, type its wording, choose its answer type, and say whether it is required. For a Yes / No or a choice question you may name a **knock-out answer** — the answer that takes a supplier out of consideration; the question then becomes required. Reorder with the arrows, remove with the bin. A list of questions can be kept as a **template** and loaded into another draft.
- **Operator — fill:** per question — *Question* (the wording); *Answer type*: Yes / No, Single choice, Multiple choice, Number with unit, Text, Document requested; *Choices (one per line)* for the two choice types, at least two different ones; *Unit* for a number; *Knock-out answer* (optional) for Yes / No and the choice types; *Required*. Templates: *Save these questions as* + **Save as template**; *Saved templates* + **Load into editor** / **Delete template**.
- **Tester — expected state:** Draft
- **Tester — confirm:** toast "Questionnaire saved on RFQ-…" / "N questions. Suppliers answer them once the event is published." The section lists the questions as Q1, Q2 … with type, Required / Optional, the choices and, where set, "Knock-out answer: …". The event is still Draft. Saving an empty list reads "The draft now asks no questionnaire." and the section returns to "This draft asks no questionnaire. Published like this, suppliers record their interest and a note at the RFI stage." After publishing, the section shows the same list with no edit button, and the answer matrix under it.
- **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_questionnaire_set`
- **Checks that can refuse:** two hooks, in this order. `rfq_questionnaire_at_rfi` — `QUESTIONNAIRE_STAGE_NOT_RFI` when the event starts at RFP or RFQ. `rfq_questionnaire_well_formed` — `QUESTIONNAIRE_MALFORMED`, naming the question and one of `NOT_A_LIST`, `ID_MISSING`, `ID_DUPLICATE`, `PROMPT_MISSING`, `TYPE_UNKNOWN`, `OPTIONS_TOO_FEW`, `UNIT_MISSING`, `KNOCKOUT_NOT_TAKEN`, `KNOCKOUT_NOT_AN_ANSWER`, `KNOCKOUT_NOT_REQUIRED`. The editor reads the same rule before the act: the first fault is named under the list ("Q2 needs at least two different choices.") and **Save questionnaire** is held until it is gone. Legality: the verb is legal on a Draft only, so on a published event it is `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION` — the questions do not move once suppliers can answer them. No named person is required, as none is to raise the draft.
- **Glossary:** `POLICY_REJECTED`, `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`, `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, `SCOPE_DENIED`.
- **Honesty:** Templates are kept in this browser's local storage only: they are not governed, not shared with colleagues, not in the audit trail, and another browser does not have them. A stored entry that is not a valid questionnaire is not listed, and the bar says how many were dropped. A knock-out answer removes nobody by itself — the buyer chooses the shortlist at the advance step. The answer matrix cannot be exported in this build, and answers are not scored. An event may be published at RFI with no questionnaire; its suppliers then record interest and a note.

### t_rfq_criteria_set — Set the RFP evaluation criteria <!-- transition:t_rfq_criteria_set -->

- **Step kind:** records a fact (state-preserving)
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:create`)
- **From → to:** Draft → Draft
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` → open a **Draft** event that starts at RFI or RFP → section **RFP evaluation criteria** → **Set criteria** (or **Edit criteria** once some are saved) → the editor → **Save criteria** (or **Cancel**, which saves nothing). The section is absent on an event that starts at RFQ. A seat without `rfq:create` sees "Awaiting Procurement" in the button's slot (`handoff-rfq-criteria`). In the wizard each start stage has one line saying what it asks and where it is written; the RFP's says the criteria are set on the draft.
- **Operator — do:** Say what a proposal will be weighed on and how heavily each thing counts. **Add criterion**, name it, give it a weight in percent, optionally place it in the **Technical** or the **Commercial** part, and say whether a response to it is required. The weights must sum to 100%: the running total is shown under the list and says when it does not.
- **Operator — fill:** per criterion — *Criterion* (its name); *Weight (%)*, above 0 and at most 100, with two decimals at most; *Part*: Neither, Technical, Commercial; *A response is required*.
- **Tester — expected state:** Draft
- **Tester — confirm:** toast "Criteria saved on RFQ-…" / "The draft weighs proposals on N criteria." The section lists the criteria as C1, C2 … with part, Required / Optional and the weight. The event is still Draft. Saving an empty list reads "The draft now sets no criteria." After publishing, the section shows the same list with no edit button, and the proposals, the score sheets and the ranking under it.
- **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_criteria_set`
- **Checks that can refuse:** three hooks, in this order. `rfq_criteria_on_rfp_path` — `CRITERIA_NO_RFP_STAGE` when the event starts at RFQ. `rfq_criteria_well_formed` — `CRITERIA_MALFORMED`, naming the criterion and one of `NOT_A_LIST`, `ID_MISSING`, `ID_DUPLICATE`, `NAME_MISSING`, `WEIGHT_INVALID`, `GROUP_UNKNOWN`. `rfq_criteria_weights_total` — `CRITERIA_WEIGHTS_NOT_100`, stating the sum, when the weights do not sum to exactly 100. The editor reads the per-criterion rule before the act: the first fault is named under the list ("C2 has no name.") and **Save criteria** is held until it is gone. The sum is not held: the line under the list reads "The weights sum to 95%, and must sum to 100%.", the press goes to the machine, and the toast reads "Not saved. The weights sum to 95%, not 100%. Change the weights until they sum to 100%." Legality: the verb is legal on a Draft only, so on a published event it is `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION` — the criteria and their weights do not move once suppliers are proposing against them. No named person is required, as none is to raise the draft.
- **Glossary:** `POLICY_REJECTED`, `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`, `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, `SCOPE_DENIED`.
- **Honesty:** A supplier is shown the criteria, their weights and their parts from the RFP stage on; it is never shown a score. An event may be published with an RFP stage and no criteria; its suppliers then record interest and a note at that stage and nothing is scored. Under "Evaluation criteria" the supplier's card lists only the criteria the buyer set, when the supplier's read carries them; where it carries none — at an RFI, or on an event that sets none — the card says "The buyer has shared no evaluation criteria for this event." Criteria are not kept as a template in this build.

### t_rfq_publish — Publish the RFQ <!-- transition:t_rfq_publish -->

- **Step kind:** operator action
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:publish`)
- **From → to:** Draft → Open
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` → open the Draft → "Lifecycle actions" → **Publish event**. A seat without the atom sees "Awaiting Procurement" in that slot (`handoff-rfq-publish`); the slot exists only on a Draft.
- **Operator — do:** Puts the request in front of the invited suppliers. Nothing is visible to them before this, so it is the moment sourcing actually begins. Read the gate notice on the panel first — it says whether the event needs competitive bidding, whether it sits exactly at the floor ("Two eligible suppliers invited. Three is the standard for a competitive event."), or whether preferred-supplier standing could not be checked — then press Publish RFQ. Toast: "RFQ-… published · Now open to N invited suppliers."
- **Operator — fill:** nothing to fill.
- **Tester — expected state:** Open
- **Tester — confirm:** the pill reads **Open** and the row moves from the All-only Draft into the **Open** tab (or **Pending Award** if the fixture already records every invitee as responded); the panel's "Lifecycle actions" now show only **Cancel event**; the timeline's "Sent to N suppliers" step completes. On the supplier side, each invited supplier's `/supplier/rfqs` ("My Sourcing Events") → **Open events** now lists the event with **Submit quote**.
- **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_publish`
- **Checks that can refuse:** four hooks, evaluated in this order. `rfq_actor_attributed` — the seat must carry a named person; a seat with nobody named is refused `RFQ_ACTOR_UNATTRIBUTED: this seat carries no person, and publishing, cancelling or awarding a sourcing event is recorded against the person who decided it. Adopt a sample user on the identity panel, then take the act again.` (the surface says so under the buttons before the act). `rfq_publish_invitees_eligible` — every invited supplier must be in a state that may be invited; today the only refusing status is **Suspended** (Onboarding suppliers may bid), and a supplier id the roster does not hold is neither refused nor counted. Refusal reads `INVITEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE: <supplier id> (Suspended) may not be invited to a sourcing event`. `rfq_publish_competition` — after eligibility, the event must have at least **2 eligible** invitees unless an exemption applies; an in-force **Mandatory** or **Sole Source** preferred-supplier listing held by any invited supplier for any material on the event removes the requirement. Refusal reads `COMPETITION_UNDER_FLOOR: N eligible supplier(s) invited, a competitive event needs at least 2`. Exactly two is allowed with the note that three is the standard; an event whose material codes no listing knows is not refused — the exemption is undecidable and the event simply competes. The fourth, last: `rfq_publish_deadline_current` — a draft whose response deadline is before the declared present (31 Aug 2026), or that states none, is refused `PUBLISH_DEADLINE_PAST`; the panel says so in the Publish slot instead of offering the button.
- **Glossary:** `INVITEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `COMPETITION_UNDER_FLOOR`, `POLICY_REJECTED`, `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`, `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
- **Honesty:** Publication is what exposes the event; it changes no other field. There is no value threshold anywhere in the tree, so every non-exempt event needs competition regardless of value. `rfq-008` (RFQ-2026-008) is a Draft with no invitees and is deliberately kept as the specimen of a draft that cannot be published — the refusal is `COMPETITION_UNDER_FLOOR` with 0 eligible, and no verb can add an invitee to it. `rfq-014` (RFQ-2026-014) publishes with the notice that competitive bidding is not required, because sup-005 holds an in-force Mandatory listing for `AI-NIAC-6601` and is invited. The exemption is decided the same whether or not the listing has been published to the supplier; the notice separately says whether the supplier has been told.

### t_rfq_close — Close bidding <!-- transition:t_rfq_close -->

- **Step kind:** operator action
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:close`)

### t_rfq_fx_pin — Record the exchange-rate basis <!-- transition:t_rfq_fx_pin -->

- **Step kind:** records a fact (state-preserving)
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:fx-pin`, deliberately distinct from `rfq:award`)
- **From → to:** Open, Closed → same state
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` → open the RFQ → "Exchange rate basis" (inside the quote comparison) → **Record USD rate** (or **Supersede USD rate** when one is in force) → dialog "Record the USD exchange rate" / "Supersede the USD exchange rate" → **Record rate** / **Record new rate**. The dialog disables its own button on a blank, non-numeric, ambiguous ("17.250"), zero, empty-date, unreadable-date or future-date entry, each with its own message. - **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_fx_pin`
- **Checks that can refuse:** `rfq_fx_pin_well_formed` — the quote currency must be a permitted bid currency (IDR, USD, EUR) and not the base IDR ("IDR is the comparison base — it has no rate to pin"); the rate must be a finite number greater than 0; `asOf` must be a readable date; `source` must be `MANUAL` or `SAP_EXHGRATE`. - **Glossary:** `FX_UNPINNED`, `FX_STALE`, `EMPTY_QTY`, `NOT_NUMERIC`, `AMBIGUOUS_QTY`, `POLICY_REJECTED`.

The age is read against the same day every deadline on the page counts from, never the browser clock, so a rate dated on the declared present is current. That is why `rfq-013`'s seeded pins — the one in force is dated 22 Aug 2026 — are stale by design and the ranked outcome is reached by recording a current rate. Every pin — manual or labelled SAP — is stored with liveness `SIMULATED`; no rate feed is connected. The recorded-at timestamp is store-assigned. A single-currency event never needs a pin. The rate is written for this verb only: the same fields carried in a request for any other verb on the event are ignored, so a rate never reaches the ledger without this verb's checks.

The cancelled event leaves their Open events list. A Draft that was never published can be cancelled without any supplier ever having seen it.

### t_rfq_reopen — Reopen a closed RFQ <!-- transition:t_rfq_reopen -->

- **Step kind:** operator action (exception path)
- **Role:** buyer · procurement (atom `rfq:reopen`)
- **From → to:** Closed → Open
- **Operator — where:** `/buyer/sourcing` → **Closed** tab → open the RFQ → "Lifecycle actions" → **Reopen event** (offered on Closed only; state-exclusive with Publish). A seat without the atom sees "Awaiting Procurement" in that slot (`handoff-rfq-reopen`).
- **Operator — do:** Reopens a finished event for further responses — too few answers, or a requirement that moved. It reuses the request rather than starting a new one. Press Reopen RFQ. Toast: "RFQ-… reopened · The sourcing event is open for responses again."
- **Operator — fill:** nothing to fill.
- **Tester — expected state:** Open
- **Tester — confirm:** the panel closes; the row leaves the Closed tab for **Open** or **Pending Award** (both seeded Closed events record every invitee as responded, so they land in Pending Award); reopening the panel now offers Cancel RFQ and, because every invitee has responded, the "Award action" section. The invited suppliers see the event again under Open events unless they already quoted it (a quoted event is pruned from that tab).
- **Tester — trigger event:** `t_rfq_reopen`
- **Checks that can refuse:** one hook since stages: `rfq_reopen_not_an_advance` — a reopen whose payload carries a shortlist is refused `REOPEN_CARRIES_SHORTLIST` (reopen takes more answers at the same stage; a shortlist is carried by **Advance**). The surface never sends one. Otherwise none beyond role, legality and required fields.
- **Glossary:** `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`, `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
- **Honesty:** Reopening does not move the response or award deadlines — both stay as authored (early July 2026 on the two fixtures), so a reopened event reads as overdue immediately and takes no new quotation: its response deadline has passed. Reopening is not needed in order to award — the award commits from Closed as well (`rfq-004` RFQ-2026-004, `rfq-005` RFQ-2026-005).

## 4 · Decision forks and exception paths

- **Advance, or conclude without award (at Closed, RFI or RFP stage).** Branch A — `t_rfq_advance` — **When:** at least one supplier responded at this stage and a shortlist of them goes forward; the event reopens at the next stage. Branch B — `t_rfq_conclude` — **When:** nobody responded, or nobody is worth carrying; the event ends Concluded with the reason. Branch C — `t_rfq_reopen` — **When:** more responses are wanted at this same stage.
- **The questionnaire is fixed at publication.** `t_rfq_questionnaire_set` is legal on a Draft only — **When:** the buyer wants different questions after publishing, there is no verb for it; the event is cancelled and raised again. This is deliberate: an answer is kept against the question it was given to.
- **The criteria are fixed at publication, and the scores at the advance.** `t_rfq_criteria_set` is legal on a Draft only; `t_rfq_proposal_score` is legal on a Closed event at its RFP stage and is refused `SCORES_LOCKED` once the event has advanced — **When:** the buyer wants different weights after publishing, or a different score after advancing, there is no verb for either. Reopening the RFP (`t_rfq_reopen`) is not an advance: after closing again the proposals are still scored.
- **A ranking, and the shortlist.** Not a transition. **When** proposals are scored, the ranking orders the suppliers by weighted total and the advance form can tick the top N or everyone at or above a total; the buyer still chooses, and the shortlist still meets the competition floor.
- **A knock-out answer, and the shortlist.** Not a transition. **When** a supplier gives the knock-out answer of a question, its response is submitted like any other; the buyer's matrix marks the cell "Knock-out answer" and the supplier "Failed a knock-out: Qn", and the advance form starts with that supplier unticked. The buyer may tick it.
- **Award, or conclude without award (RFQ stage).** Branch A — `t_rfq_award` — **When:** one quotation is chosen. Branch B — `t_rfq_conclude` — **When:** none is acceptable; every quotation still being weighed is withdrawn. An award at RFI or RFP is refused `AWARD_STAGE_NOT_RFQ`.
- **A draft past its response deadline.** `t_rfq_publish` is refused `PUBLISH_DEADLINE_PAST`, and no verb edits a deadline — **When:** a draft sat unpublished until its deadline went by (`rfq-017`); its only exit is `t_rfq_cancel`. The wizard refuses a past deadline, so a new draft cannot be raised in that state.
- **Publish or cancel (at Draft).** Branch A — `t_rfq_publish` — **When:** the invited set passes both gates (no Suspended invitee; at least two eligible invitees, or an in-force Mandatory / Sole Source listing on an invited supplier for a material of the event). Branch B — `t_rfq_cancel` — **When:** the requirement went away, or the draft can never be published — `rfq-008` has no invitees and no verb can add one, so cancel is its only exit.
- **Award, pin, cancel or wait (at Open).** Branch A — `t_rfq_award` — **When:** at least one quotation was received and one is chosen; the winner and the losers are decided in the same act, after a second ask. Branch B — `t_rfq_fx_pin` — **When:** the comparison refuses `FX_UNPINNED` or `FX_STALE`; the RFQ stays Open and the pin is appended. Branch C — `t_rfq_cancel` — **When:** the event is abandoned before award. Branch D — `t_rfq_close` — **When:** the buyer stops taking quotations; the event can then be awarded, reopened or cancelled.
- **Reopen, award, pin or cancel (at Closed).** Branch A — `t_rfq_reopen` — **When:** more responses are wanted, or the buyer wants to award (the award panel is Open-only on the surface). Branch B — `t_rfq_award` — legal in the machine from Closed; not offered on the buyer surface. Branch C — `t_rfq_fx_pin` — **When:** a rate is recorded on a Closed event; the state is preserved. Branch D — `t_rfq_cancel` — **When:** the closed event will not be awarded.
- **The award fan-out.** After `t_rfq_award` succeeds, the platform dispatches `t_quotation_award` for the chosen quotation and `t_quotation_reject` for every other quotation on the event, under the automation grant. A sibling that is already Awarded or Rejected is refused `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION` and the refusal is recorded with the award's `causationId`; the award itself is never undone by a failed sibling.
- **The requisition cascade.** After `t_rfq_create` succeeds with a `sourceRequisitionId`, the platform dispatches `t_pr_source` on that requisition. **When** the requisition is Approved it moves to Sourcing Event with the new RFQ number as its linked document; **when** it is in any other state the cascade is refused `ILLEGAL_TRANSITION` and recorded; **when** no requisition was chosen (the common case) nothing is dispatched.
- **The competition floor is a wizard gate too.** The same decision the publish hooks take is rendered on wizard step 2 and on the panel: an ineligible invitee or fewer than two eligible invitees stops the step from advancing (the checkboxes stay live so the set can be corrected); exactly two advances with the note that three is the standard; an exempt event advances with one invitee.

## 7 · Status history

Every dispatch writes one `TransitionEvent`: `event` = the transition id, `actor` = `buyer:all` for a buyer seat (every RFQ verb is a buyer verb), `ts`, `outcome` (`done` / `failed`), `correlationId` (`cmd_…`), and `causationId` on a cascaded event — the correlationId of the command that caused it. The award fan-out runs under the automation grant with the same buyer scope, so its events also carry `actor` = `buyer:all`, distinguishable from the award only by their `causationId` and by the absence of an attribution. A `failed` event carries the refusal `reason`. Human acts also carry the session's actor attribution: the sample person adopted on the identity panel, or `UNATTRIBUTED: NO_PERSON_IN_SESSION` — on which publish, cancel and award are refused.
